# Global Literature Craft — Writing Master Reference

## Policy
**Techniques only. No style mimicry, no lifted passages, no copyrighted text.**  
All rules below are abstract methods derived from close reading. Apply them to original stories only.

---

## Core Principles from Nobel-Level Authors

### 1. Sadness Is a Door, Not a Wall
**Source concept:** Ishiguro / Maass — grief is poignant when the thing lost is shown alive first.

**Abstract rule:**  
Before hurting a character, show them loving something specific.  
The stronger the attachment, the deeper the wound.

**Do not:** copy Ishiguro's restrained British voice.  
**Do:** create your own scene where a character's small daily ritual gets interrupted.

---

### 2. Kinetic Dialogue (The Unspoken Layer)
**Source concept:** Ishiguro / Murakami — characters protect themselves with silence.

**Abstract rule:**  
- Insert pauses, subject changes, unfinished sentences.  
- Let subtext carry the scene.  
- The most important line is often the one not spoken.

**Do not:** mimic Murakami's "I" narrator or his surreal asides.  
**Do:** write your own POV character's evasion patterns.

---

### 3. Specific Materiality
**Source concept:** Ishiguro / McCarthy — emotion needs a physical anchor.

**Abstract rule:**  
Replace "she was sad" with a concrete, degraded object.  
Sensory texture = reader embodiment.

**Do not:** copy McCarthy's landscapes or Ishiguro's butlers.  
**Do:** pick one small worn thing from your character's world per emotional beat.

---

### 4. Negative Space Pacing
**Source concept:** Shirley Jackson / Ellison / Murakami — dread lives in the pause.

**Abstract rule:**  
After revelation, insert silence. Let environment carry emotion.  
Long corridor of nothing > monologue.

**Do not:** copy Murakami's wells or Jackson's haunted houses.  
**Do:** find your setting's equivalent empty space.

---

### 5. Identity Through Micro-Attitudes
**Source concept:** Munro / Chekhov — character is choice under pressure.

**Abstract rule:**  
Show who someone is through one small, repeated gesture.  
Emergency reveals omission, not action.

**Do not:** mimic Munro's rural Ontario or Chekhov's country estates.  
**Do:** observe your character's default small action and repeat it.

---

### 6. Moral Stance as Emotional Lever
**Source concept:** Maass / Tolkien — highest emotions are moral emotions.

**Abstract rule:**  
Give character a moment choosing the hard right over the easy wrong.  
Make reader admire them before breaking their heart.

**Do not:** copy Tolkien's mythic scale or his "eucatastrophe" structure.  
**Do:** give your character a personal ethical test.

---

### 7. Memory as Unreliable Cartographer
**Source concept:** Ishiguro / Robinson — memory is meaning-making, not accuracy.

**Abstract rule:**  
Let two characters remember the same event differently.  
Use memory gaps to create dramatic irony.

**Do not:** copy Ishiguro's English country house narrator.  
**Do:** create your own character's selective remembering.

---

### 8. The Body Speaks First
**Source concept:** Munro / Weil / Levine — emotion is somatic before cognitive.

**Abstract rule:**  
Locate feeling in the body before naming it.  
Cold toes, tightened chest, involuntary tremor.

**Do not:** copy Munro's bodily detail style.  
**Do:** find your character's unique physical response and describe it freshly.

## Harry Potter — Abstract Structural Lessons
**Reference:** J.K. Rowling, Harry Potter series  
**Status:** STUDY ONLY — no prose mimicry, no lifted scenes

| Technique | Abstract Rule | Application |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Ordinary World + Hidden Magic | Establish a relatable "normal" first, then reveal hidden layer | In non-magical stories: hidden layer = hidden past, hidden grief, hidden talent |
| Object as Secret | A single prop carries emotional weight (glasses, wand, snitch) | Give your story one recurring object that changes meaning each appearance |
| Found Family | Characters who start as strangers choose each other | Show the choice moment, not just the result |
| Humor Under Pressure | Comic detail during dark scenes prevents emotional fatigue | Insert one small absurdity right before/after a devastating moment |
| The Choice Moment | Character must choose between safety and love/duty | Moral test at chapter end; let the choice have cost |
| Pacing Through Calendar | Events gathered around recurring dates create rhythm | Use a recurring meeting/day/time as structural spine |
| Name as Identity | Characters redefine themselves by rejecting or embracing names | Let a character rename themselves or reject a given name |

**Do not copy:** boarding school structure, British boarding slang, "boy who lived" arc, Quidditch, house rivalries.  
**Do extract:** how to build a secret world inside a known world; how to make an object sacred through repetition.

## Marquez / Murakami / Mishima — Abstract Lessons Only

### Marquez
- **Macondo as microcosm:** one fictional place containing all themes → apply by making your setting a closed system
- **Cyclical time:** same patterns repeat across generations → apply by showing character repeating family patterns
- **Matter-of-fact miracle:** extraordinary thing stated in flat prose → apply by writing your impossible moment like a weather report

### Murakami
- **Well as threshold:** vertical space = unconscious → apply by giving your story a vertical hiding place
- **Jazz as mood:** music carries unspoken history → apply by giving character one song that means everything
- **Missing person as ghost:** absence more present than presence → apply by writing the ghost, not the funeral

### Mishima
- **Body as altar:** physical perfection/coordination mirrors spiritual state → apply by giving character a body discipline
- **Beauty as violence:** aesthetic moment contains threat → apply by making beautiful scenes slightly dangerous
- **Mask as identity:** character performs role they hate → apply by giving character a public face they cannot remove
