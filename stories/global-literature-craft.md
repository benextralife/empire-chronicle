# Global Literature Craft — Writing Master Reference

## Core Principles from Nobel-Level Authors

### 1. Sadness Is a Door, Not a Wall
Donald Maass / Ishiguro: Grief is poignant when life is made beautiful first. The stronger the hope, the deeper the wound.

**In practice:**
- Before hurting a character, show them loving something.
- Before killing a hope, show the hope alive.
- Reader attachment = emotional devastation later.

### 2. Kinetic Dialogue
Ishiguro / Murakami: Characters say less than they mean. The most powerful dialogue is not what is spoken but what is being protected from speech.

**In practice:**
- Insert silences in dialogue.
- Let one character change the subject when they are about to crack.
- Subtext > explicit confession.

### 3. Specific Materiality
Kazuo Ishiguro / Haruki Murakami: Emotion is grounded in physical detail — a frayed cord, a violin's varnish worn to tobacco colour, smudged margins.

**In practice:**
- Replace "she was sad" with a concrete detail.
- Use degraded objects to mark time and loss.
- Sensory texture anchors catharsis.

### 4. Negative Space Pacing
Murakami / Ellison / Shirley Jackson: The most terrifying moment is not the explosion — it is the five seconds before it.

**In practice:**
- After revelation, insert silence.
- Let the environment carry the emotion.
- A long corridor of nothing can punch harder than any monologue.

### 5. Identity Through Micro-Attitudes
Subtle gesture, not declaration: a hand hovering over a phone, a hand that does not hug, a voice that chooses music instead of words.

**In practice:**
- Show who a character has become through one small choice.
- Repetition of micro-attitude becomes character signature.
- People reveal themselves in emergencies through omission, not action.

### 6. Moral Stance as Emotional Lever
Donald Maass: The highest emotions are moral emotions — sacrifice, selflessness, integrity.

**In practice:**
- Give characters moments where they choose the hard right over the easy wrong.
- Make the reader admire them before breaking their hearts.
- Catharsis requires respect before ruin.

### 7. Memory as Unreliable Cartographer
Ishiguro: Memory is not accuracy; it is meaning-making. What a character remembers differently from what happened carries emotional truth.

**In practice:**
- Let two characters remember the same event differently.
- Let a character misremember on purpose.
- Use memory gaps to create dramatic irony.

### 8. The Body Speaks Before the Mind
Somatic writing tradition (Levine / Weil / Munro): Emotion is physical first — cold toes, a tightened chest, a hand that will not stop trembling.

**In practice:**
- Locate emotion in the body before naming it.
- Use involuntary physical responses.
- Skip "she felt sad" and go straight to the body.
