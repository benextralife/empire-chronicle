Chapter 2: Director Hale

Director Hale read the seismic report twice before he called Maren to his office. His office was a converted storage closet on Level 3, crammed with datapads, geological surveys, and a single framed photograph of Earth's coastlines — blue and green, designed to remind anyone who looked at it that there had once been a choice, that the universe had not always narrowed down to red dust and recycled air.

"Maren." He did not stand. He gestured at the empty chair opposite his desk. "You wrote this?"

She nodded.

"I want you to walk me through it."

She did, careful and precise, the way her geology professor at Elysium Academy had taught her. Every tremor, every particle count, every harmonic anomaly. She had practiced this presentation in her head a hundred times, but standing in front of Hale, she felt small. Hale had run this colony for twenty-two years. He had survived two dust storms that had killed forty people. He had negotiated water rights with the Belt syndicates when Mars was barely a footnote on their charts. If anyone could handle what she was about to tell him, it was Director Hale.

He listened without interrupting, his face growing grimmer with each sentence. When she finished, he stood and walked to the window, looking out at the rust-colored plain that stretched to the horizon. A mining crawler moved slowly across the surface, its headlights twin pinpricks in the permanent twilight of the dome's interior lighting.

"How long?" he asked.

"Best estimate: three days before the domes start experiencing structural stress. Seven days before the atmospheric pressure exceeds safe operating limits. Two weeks before the aquifers become undrinkable." Maren paused. "If the seismic activity continues at this rate."

Hale turned. "It will continue. Geological momentum is not something we can stop with a vote or a prayer."

"Then we evacuate."

"To where?" Hale's voice was not cruel, merely factual. He had asked himself this question a hundred times, she realized, and still had no answer. "Earth? The Belt? Luna? We have no ships large enough to move twelve thousand people safely. And even if we did, no one is going to accept twelve thousand refugees with no resources and no welcome. Mars was supposed to be the future. No one wants the past showing up on their doorstep."

Maren felt a coldness that had nothing to do with the dome's regulated temperature. She had grown up on Mars, like everyone her age. Earth was a photograph, a story, a myth. Luna was for rich tourists. The Belt was for criminals and engineers. There was nowhere to go.

"Then we fix it," she said.

Hale laughed, short and sharp. "Fix a planet? We built a few domes and drilled a few wells. We don't fix planets. We don't even understand them."

He sat back down and pulled up a screen. "Your data confirmed what I already suspected. The Tharsis event is not a quake. It's a collapse. The mantle beneath the bulge is draining into the core, causing the crust to subside. It's been happening for millennia, but the rate has accelerated since the Gates opened."

Maren frowned. "The Gates?"

"You know about the Architects." Hale's voice dropped. "The Gates they left on Mars are not just doorways. They're pumps. The Architects were drawing energy from the planet's core — enough to power interstellar travel for millions of years. They depleted it. Mars didn't die of natural causes. It was bled dry."

The room felt very small. Maren thought of the blue Gates, pulsing with harmonic light near Olympus Mons, beautiful and terrible and completely out of human control. She had been raised to believe the Gates were humanity's greatest inheritance. Now she understood they were its executioner.

"How long?" she asked again.

Hale looked at the seismic readout. "Two weeks until the first dome breaches. Three until this entire region becomes uninhabitable."

"Then we tell them. The colony. We give everyone a choice."

Hale shook his head. "Panic kills more people than geology. I cannot allow that."

"People deserve to know."

"People deserve to survive." Hale leaned forward. "I need you on my team, Maren. Not because you're my daughter's friend. Because you understand what's happening better than any of my senior staff. Help me manage the crisis. Quietly. Protocol by protocol."

Maren looked at the framed photograph of Earth on his wall. She thought of her own family, buried in the domes of Elysium, who believed Mars would outlive them. She thought of the red dust outside, singing its song of collapse. She thought of Hale's eyes, pleading and terrified behind the Director's mask.

She nodded. "I'll help."
