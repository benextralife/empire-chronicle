Chapter 4: The Council Session

The Council chamber was built for four hundred people. On the morning of the seismic crisis, it held every soul who could fit inside, plus another thousand crowding the corridors and stairwells, listening on speakers, watching on screens, desperate for any news that wasn't already in their heads. The air smelled of sweat, recycled oxygen, and the kind of fear that only comes when a community realizes its leaders have been lying.

Director Hale stood at the podium. He looked older than Maren had ever seen him. His hands shook slightly as he adjusted the microphone, just once, as if trying to steady himself before he said the thing that would end the world as they knew it.

"People of Elysium," he began. "I have called this session to address the events of the past week. The tremors. The dust. The aquifer readings."

The room was silent. A few people coughed. The ventilation system hummed.

"We have completed our analysis." Hale's voice was steady. "The Tharsis plateau is undergoing structural collapse. The cause is the Architects' Gates, which have been drawing thermal energy from Mars's core for two hundred million years. That energy is now depleted. The mantle is destabilizing. The crust is moving. Within fourteen days, this dome will no longer be habitable."

The silence broke into chaos. People shouted. Someone fainted. A woman near the front began screaming at Hale to do something, anything, to stop it. A man in the back started praying out loud, his voice rising over the cacophony like smoke. Maren pressed her back against the wall and watched, her hands trembling, as the colony unraveled in real time.

Hale waited. When the noise subsided to a manageable murmur, he continued, his voice barely audible over the ragged breathing of twelve thousand frightened people.

"We have identified four transport vessels that can evacuate approximately eight thousand people. Our current population is twelve thousand."

More shouting. A man threw something — a datapad, Maren thought — that struck the podium with a crack. Security moved in. Hale held up a hand, and they stopped.

"We are not leaving anyone behind if we can help it." Hale leaned into the microphone. "We are requesting assistance from Earth, Luna, and the Belt. We are also exploring alternative solutions, including temporary habitat expansion and emergency aquifer drilling. We will build twelve new modules before the dome collapses if we have to work around the clock to do it."

Alternative solutions. Maren knew what he meant. Drilling into the mantle, reinforcing the crust, building new domes fast enough to matter. It was technically possible, but it would require every hand on Mars working eighteen-hour days for two weeks straight, with no guarantee of success. She also knew Hale would attempt it. He would rather die trying than admit defeat.

When the session finally ended, the Council had agreed to begin emergency construction on twelve new habitat modules, using prefabricated materials stockpiled for expansion. The work would start at dawn. No one spoke about the evacuation ships. No one mentioned that eight thousand was a hard ceiling.

Maren found Lira in the medical bay, supervising the sorting of emergency supplies. Lira looked up when Maren entered, her face tight with the controlled fear of someone who had spent her whole life preparing for exactly this kind of crisis.

"He told them," Lira said.

"Everything."

"And they agreed to work."

"Of course they agreed." Maren looked around the bay. Nurses moved with efficient urgency. Respirators were stacked in crates. A row of med-bots hummed in the corner, their lights blinking soft green. "What else could they do?"

"Rebel." Lira closed the datapad in her hand. "Panic. Run. Give up. Some of the outer domes are already being abandoned. People are choosing to leave rather than wait for a miracle that might never come."

"Can you blame them?" Maren asked.

"No." Lira hesitated, then reached across the distance between them and placed her hand on Maren's arm. Her touch was warm, human, and utterly insufficient against the weight of what was coming. "Neither can I. We have fourteen days. Maybe less. Build the domes, or bury the ones we have."
