Chapter 2: The Earth Directorate

Admiral Helena Cross stood before the United Earth Council with a holographic display of Mars rotating between her hands. She was fifty-two, Mars-born, and the first officer from the outer colonies to command Earth's combined fleet. Her uniform was immaculate. Her patience was not.

"Report," said Council President Tanaka.

"Mars Station has reported an anomalous signal," Cross said. "Olympus Mons. Sector seven. Detailed scans are blocked by an unknown energy field."

"Unknown energy field," Tanaka repeated. "On Mars."

"Yes, sir."

The council chamber was located in Geneva, in a building designed to remind humanity of its unity. Steel arches shaped like branches reaching toward each other. Glass walls showing green gardens. The architecture said "peace." The faces around the table said "survival."

"And the Terraform Corporation?" asked Councilwoman Park.

"Director Kael has declared martial law on Mars," Cross said. "Claims the discovery falls under corporate sovereign rights. He's mobilizing private security forces."

"Private security," Park snorted. "You mean mercenaries."

"I mean ten thousand heavily armed contractors with orbital drop capabilities." Cross turned off the hologram and faced the council directly. "We have three hours before Kael's forces reach the excavation site. After that, whatever was found on Mars becomes Terraform Corp property."

"And if the corporation claims a weapon?" Tanaka asked.

"Then we have a war. Not between nations, but between humanity's last government and its most powerful company."

The room grew quiet. Outside the glass walls, a spring rain fell on Geneva — rain that had taken seventy years of terraforming to create. Everyone in the room knew how fragile that rain was. How fragile everything was.

"Admiral," said General Osei, head of Earth Defense Forces. "What does your intelligence say about this... anomaly?"

"Dr. Elena Voss. Lead archaeologist at Olympus Mons. Her publications suggest she believes Mars was once inhabited. She's been arguing for years that the planet's geological features aren't natural — that they're engineered."

"She's a conspiracy theorist," Park said.

"She's also the most brilliant xenogeologist Earth has ever produced. And she was the first person to respond to the signal." Cross stood up. "I recommend we deploy Fleet Wing Seven to Mars orbit immediately. Not as an invasion force. As a deterrent."

"Against a corporation?" Tanaka asked.

"Against whoever tries to weaponize whatever is buried under Olympus Mons."

The council voted forty-three to seven in favor of Cross's deployment. Seven dissents came from representatives with significant Terraform Corp stock holdings.

As she left the chamber, Cross received a priority transmission from Mars.

It was from Dr. Elena Voss.

The message contained a single image: a doorway made of black glass, glowing with blue light and surrounded by dust. But the image had been taken from inside the doorway, looking out.

And looking out, Dr. Voss had photographed a fleet.

Not a human fleet.

Something else was arriving.

Something that had been waiting two hundred million years.

Something that had built the Lumin Gate.
