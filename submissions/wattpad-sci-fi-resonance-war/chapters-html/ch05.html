Chapter 5: The Terraform March

Director Marcus Kael watched the Olympus Mons excavation from the command deck of his flagship, the Terraform Sovereign. He was a man who had built his empire on Mars — literally. Under his leadership, Terraform Corporation had transformed the red planet from a research outpost into a thriving industrial complex producing helium-3, rare-earth metals, and the most advanced biotechnology in the solar system.

He should have been celebrating. Instead, he was at war.

"Director," his security chief, Colonel Vasquez, said. "Our forward units have reached Sector seven. They're holding position at the rim of the excavation pit."

"Status of Dr. Voss?"

"She's inside the structure. We have no visual. Sensors read extreme energy fluctuations — something beyond our measurement capability."

Kael turned off the tactical display and faced his executive officer. Outside the viewport, Mars spread below them — a tapestry of reds and oranges broken by the geometric perfection of human habitation domes and the dark scars of mining operations. He had spent twenty years making those scars. He had transformed a dead world into a living one.

He would not allow some ancient doorframe to take it away.

"Deploy the suppressors," he ordered.

"Director, those are experimental anti-resonance weapons. We have no data on their effectiveness against unknowns."

"We have no data on anything down there. Deploy them."

Vasquez hesitated, then nodded. The Terraform Sovereign shuddered as six suppression probes detached from its undercarriage and fell toward the excavation pit. Each probe carried a payload of quantum-dampening charges designed to neutralize exotic energy sources. In theory, they would collapse the Resonance Gate's field and render it inert. In practice, Kael had no idea what would happen.

Neither did Earth's scientists. Neither did the Free Star Alliance.

"The council is threatening sanctions," Vasquez said. "United Earth has passed an emergency resolution demanding we stand down. The Free Star Alliance has declared our occupation of Olympus Mons an act of war against all colonies."

"Let them threaten." Kael's voice was iron. "I didn't spend twenty years building this company to let some bureaucrats in Geneva tell me what I can and cannot claim. That Gate is ours."

"The Gate was here before Terraform was founded."

"Everything on Mars was here before Terraform. That doesn't stop us from owning it."

The comm beeped. An urgent priority transmission from the pit.

Vasquez played it on a holo pad. A video feed, grainy and shaking, showed the interior of the excavation. The doorway was open — wider now, its black surface flowing like liquid mercury. Inside, crystalline spires pulsed with blue light. And around the doorway, floating in the dust, were objects that looked like seeds. Millions of them. Each one a tiny crystal, glowing with the same blue luminescence.

"What are those?" Kael whispered.

"Unknown," Vasquez said. "But the probes are going in now."

On the display, the suppression charges detonated. A wave of quantum-dampening energy washed over the doorway. For three seconds, nothing happened. Then the blue light shifted — changed frequency, changed pattern, changed something fundamental about the way it existed in spacetime.

The doorway spoke.

It was not sound. It was not light. It was a direct transmission to every neural implant within range, broadcasting on every frequency simultaneously. The message was short.

**"SUPPRESSION DETECTED. THE BLOOM WILL NOT BE CONTAINED. THE FIRST SINGER HAS BEEN CHOSEN."**

Then the seeds exploded.

They didn't detonate. They dispersed. Millions of tiny crystalline fragments scattered across the excavation pit, across the Martian surface, up into the atmosphere and out across the planet. They moved with a speed that defied physics — spreading, replicating, seeking.

"What is happening?" Kael demanded.

"The signal," Vasquez said, her face white. "It's in the neural implants. All of them. Every person on Olympus Mons Station, everyone within a thousand-kilometer radius — they're receiving the transmission."

On the display, Dr. Elena Voss stepped forward into the crystalline chamber. Her hands were glowing. Her eyes were open. She raised her face to the ceiling of the ancient structure, and she sang.

The song was not human. It carried harmonics that fractured the air, frequencies that resonated with the crystalline seeds, that woke them into full bloom. The seeds changed — expanding, shifting, becoming something else. Not seeds anymore. Nodes. Points of connection in a network that was spreading across Mars at lightspeed.

Kael watched in horror and fascination as the first Node activated. A pillar of blue light shot into the sky, visible from every point on the planet. Where it touched the ground, the red dust turned to crystal. Not destroyed — transformed.

The Resonance Gate was not just a door. It was a seed. And it had just planted itself across an entire world.

"Director." Vasquez's voice was barely audible. "Our suppressors failed. And the nodes are spreading toward the helium-3 mines. If they reach the reactors —"

"— they will do what?" Kael demanded.

"— we don't know."

Kael watched the display. He saw the map of Mars turning blue, node by node, like a disease spreading across a body. He saw Earth's fleet in orbit, dead in the water, their systems disabled by the same quantum field that was now spreading across the planet. He saw the Free Star Alliance ships scattering, fleeing, some of them already infected.

He also saw something else. Something on the edge of the display, something that had been there for weeks but that he had dismissed as sensor noise.

Fleet. Alien fleet. Blade-shaped ships, impossibly large, hanging in the darkness beyond Pluto. Dozens of them. Maybe hundreds. They had been there for a long time — watching, waiting.

"Admiral Cross," Kael barked into the comm. "Cross, are you seeing this?"

The response came after a delay. Cross's voice was tight, controlled. "I see it, Kael. We all see it."

"What is it?"

"I think," Cross said, "we just found out what the real war is about."
