Chapter 3: The Free Star Alliance

Captain Jax Rainer watched the approach vectors on his bridge display and wished he was anywhere else.

The ghost ship Wanderlust had been a Free Star Alliance vessel for eleven years. She had been a blockade runner, a smuggler, a diplomat's shuttle, and occasionally a pirate ship, depending on who was asking and how much credit was involved. Jax had bought her from a collapsing orbital dock on Titan with three kilograms of rare-earth isotopes and a favor he still owed to a crime lord in the Belt. She was fast, ugly, and had more hidden compartments than he could remember.

Right now she was carrying seventeen refugees from the Mars labor riots, three stolen Terraform Corp data cores, and the most wanted woman in three solar systems.

"Captain." His first officer, Kira, pointed at the sensor display. "We have company."

Jax squinted at the readout. "How many?"

"Six. Earth Directorate destroyers. They're hailing us."

"Let them hail. Put it on screen."

The holographic display flickered, and Admiral Helena Cross appeared. Her uniform was crisp, her expression unreadable. Behind her, Jax could see the command deck of the Daedalus — one of Earth's most powerful warships, her hull still bearing the scorch marks from the Resource Wars.

"Captain Rainer," Cross said. "Or should I say, Captain Rainer of the notorious Wanderlust?"

"Admiral." Jax leaned back in his command chair — a recycled pilot's seat from a decommissioned atmospheric shuttle. "To what do I owe the pleasure? Last I checked, Earth's navy was busy pretending the Free Star Alliance doesn't exist."

"We're here for Dr. Elena Voss."

Jax's hand moved almost imperceptibly toward the weapons console. "Dr. Voss is a civilian passenger on my ship. She's seeking asylum under Alliance law. Last I checked, civilians have the right to seek asylum."

"She is wanted for unauthorized contact with an alien artifact, conspiracy to weaponize extrasolar technology, and collaboration with corporate paramilitary forces during the Mars labor unrest."

"She's also the only person who knows what that artifact actually does." Jax felt the familiar rush of adrenaline that came with negotiation — the knowledge that one wrong word could turn a standoff into a firefight. "I'm not going to hand her over to a government that already has one foot in Terraform Corp's pocket."

Cross's jaw tightened. "The Directorate is offering full immunity. Every scientific resource she needs. Protection."

"Provided she hands over the Resonance Gate to your scientists."

"The Gate is on Mars, Captain. We're here to take Dr. Voss to a secure location where she can brief us."

"Martinez." Jax turned to his comm officer. "How long before those destroyers can fire without disabling our engines?"

"Forty seconds," Martinez said. "If we jump to FTL now —"

"The FTL drive is still cooling from our last jump," Kira interrupted. "Eight minutes minimum."

Cross was watching them. Her eyes flicked to Kira, then back to Jax. "You have a Free Star Alliance tactical officer on a ship that was stolen from a Belt smuggler. This is not the image you want to project, Captain."

"I'm not here to project images. I'm here to keep my passengers alive." Jax leaned forward. "Here's the deal. Dr. Voss will speak to your scientists, but under Alliance jurisdiction. Full diplomatic immunity. And in exchange, you lift your blockade on Ceres."

Cross's jaw tightened. "You're asking for a lot."

"I'm not asking. I'm negotiating. And time's running out." He glanced at the sensor feed. The destroyers were powering up their weapons. "Your call, Admiral."

Cross studied him for a long moment. Then she cut the transmission.

"Jax." Kira's voice was quiet. "Explain to me why we're doing this. We have seventeen refugees and three stolen data cores. We should be running, not negotiating with Earth's finest."

"Because the artifact on Mars isn't human." Jax stood up. "And if we fight each other over it, we'll all lose. Earth, Alliance, Belt — everyone."

Behind the Wanderlust, something large and silent dropped out of subspace.

It was not a destroyer. It was not human. And it was definitely not friendly.

The new arrival was at least two kilometers long, shaped like a blade of obsidian glass, and moving without visible propulsion. Its surface features were minimal — no windows, no ports, no visible weapons mounts. It carried a signature of absolute zero temperature that made every sensor on the bridge scream.

"Report!" Jax barked.

"Sensor ghost!" Martinez yelled. "It's there and it's not there — our readings are contradicting themselves!"

Kira was already working her console, her fingers flying across the holographic interface. "It's using some kind of subspace cloak. I can't get a lock. Captain, I've never seen anything like this."

The alien ship fired.

Not with lasers. Not with torpedoes. With silence — a wave of absolute entropy that expanded from its hull faster than any weapon physics allowed. When it touched the lead Earth destroyer, the ship simply stopped functioning. Engines died. Systems went dark. Communications went silent. The destroyer drifted, intact but lifeless, like a carcass picked clean by invisible birds.

Then the second destroyer died. Then the third.

"FTL status?" Jax shouted.

"Two minutes! We're too close to the blast radius —"

"Reroute all non-essential power! Get us out of here!"

The Wanderlust shuddered as her FTL drive spun up. Jax watched the viewscreen, his hands gripping the command chair. The alien ship remained motionless, its blade-form catching starlight that refracted into colors his eyes couldn't name. It wasn't attacking the Wanderlust. It was watching. Studying. Waiting.

Then, as suddenly as it had appeared, the alien ship turned and accelerated away, leaving a trail of displaced spacetime in its wake. It vanished into darkness before Jax could get a sensor lock.

The bridge was silent.

"Status," Jax said finally.

"Three Earth destroyers disabled," Kira reported. "No casualties reported yet. The other three are retreating. Admiral Cross is demanding we explain what just happened."

Jax looked at the empty space where the alien ship had been. "Tell her we're on our way to Mars. And tell her to have Dr. Voss ready."

"Why?" Kira asked.

"Because whatever that was." Jax nodded at the darkness. "It came from the Resonance Gate. And I don't think it's the last one."
