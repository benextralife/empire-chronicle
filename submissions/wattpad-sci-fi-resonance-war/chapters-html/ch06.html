Chapter 6: The First Singer

Elena Voss sang because she had no choice.

The Chorus was inside her now — not as sound, not as thought, but as a pulse that moved through her veins like mercury. She stood in the center of the crystalline chamber, surrounded by spires that reached up into darkness, and she felt the Architects' song vibrating through every cell of her body.

She was not just hearing it. She was part of it.

The seeds had dispersed across Mars. She could feel them — millions of tiny crystal nodes spreading through the regolith, through the atmosphere, through the water ice at the poles. Each one a point of connection. Each one singing a different note in the same chord. Together, they formed a network. A mind. A message.

The message was not for humanity. It was for the Architects' ancient enemies — the Void Walkers, the civilization that had destroyed the Architects two hundred million years ago and was now returning to finish what it had started.

Humanity was not the target. Humanity was the alarm.

"Dr. Voss." The voice came from the chamber's depths — not through comms, not through air, but through the Chorus itself. "You have activated the Bloom. You have opened the Resonance Gates. Do you understand what you have done?"

"I started a war," Elena said aloud, her voice carrying harmonics that made the crystalline spires shiver. "I turned Mars into a battlefield."

"You turned Mars into a door. And what comes through that door will test humanity's right to exist."

Elena closed her eyes. The Chorus showed her images — not memories, but visions of futures that could still be prevented. She saw Earth burning. She saw the Free Star Alliance's colonies consumed by darkness. She saw the Shadow Empire's crystalline fortresses crumbling like glass.

She saw the Void Walkers — beings of pure entropy that existed between dimensions, feeding on structured matter, leaving nothing behind but void. They had destroyed the Architects. They would destroy humanity unless something stopped them.

"What do you want from me?" she asked.

"Nothing. The Bloom wants nothing. It is a gift. But gifts can be weapons in the wrong hands." The voice shifted — less Architect, more Shadow Empire. Veyra's consciousness, flowing through the Chorus network, had found her. "We need your help, Dr. Voss. Not to fight the Void Walkers. To prepare humanity."

"Prepare how?"

"By spreading the Bloom. Not as a weapon. As a bridge. The Bloom allows human minds to connect directly to the Chorus — to hear its frequencies, to understand its mathematics, to become singers instead of listeners. If enough humans are transformed, if enough singers arise, humanity will have a chance to stand against what's coming."

"And if we don't?"

"Then the Void Walkers will consume Earth within six months. Mars will fall first. Then the Belt colonies. Then the outer systems. Humanity will be erased."

Elena opened her eyes. The crystalline chamber was brighter now, the blue light pulsing in rhythm with her heartbeat. She could feel the network spreading — nodes activating across Mars, each one a voice in the Chorus. She could feel the humans on the station, their neural implants burning with the Bloom's initial infection. Some were screaming. Some were silent. Some were already beginning to understand.

She thought of Commander Reyes, who had tried to protect her. She thought of Dr. Park, who had called her a romantic and then followed her into the excavation pit. She thought of her parents, dead in the dome collapse, who had never lived to see Mars become what it was becoming.

"I need to talk to Earth," she said.

"Earth is already receiving the transmission." Veyra's presence shifted again, becoming less alien, more human. "Admiral Cross has arrived in orbit. The Alliance ship is docked at the station. Director Kael's mercenaries are retreating. Your signal has disrupted their command structure — the Bloom affects technology as well as biology."

Elena stepped toward the doorway. The black glass had become transparent again, showing not the crystalline spires but something new: a starfield, dense with unfamiliar constellations, and a single planet at the center — blue, green, alive.

"Where is that?" she whispered.

"The Architects' homeworld. Thalassa. It still exists. The Void Walkers haven't found it yet."

"Can we get there?"

"That depends on how fast you can teach humanity to sing."
