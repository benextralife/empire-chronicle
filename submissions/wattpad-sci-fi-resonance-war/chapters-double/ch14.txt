Chapter 14: The Long Road to Thalassa

The Stillness Highway from Mars to Alpha Centauri took eleven days.

To the humans aboard the fleet, it felt like stepping through a doorway — one moment they were in Mars orbit, surrounded by the cold geometry of human warships and the blue glow of the Resonance Gates, and the next they were in a system no human eye had ever seen.

Alpha Centauri B hung above them like a pale golden coin. Its planets were unfamiliar, unnamed on any human chart. But one of them — the fourth from the star, a blue-green marble wrapped in white clouds — carried a signature that made Elena's neural implant sing.

Thalassa.

"Home," Jax said, standing beside her on the  Wanderlust'  s bridge.

"Home for the Architects," Elena corrected. "For us, it's new territory."

The fleet spread out as it entered the system —214 human ships, plus the three Shadow Empire vessels Kael had brought. They fanned out in a defensive formation, scanners sweeping for threats. There were none. Thalassa's system was quiet, peaceful, almost pastoral.

Too peaceful.

"Captain." Kira's voice had an edge. "I'm picking up something. Not a ship. Not a planet. Something… vast. At the system's outer edge."

Jax leaned over her console. The sensor display showed a structure so large it registered as a shadow against the starfield. It was not a ship. It was a wall. A barrier wrapping the entire system, constructed from materials that absorbed all radiation, all light, all detection.

"The Architects' outer defense," Elena said. "They built it to hide Thalassa from the Void Walkers."

"And now it's hiding something else." Jax pointed at a secondary reading. "There's a fleet inside. Not human. Not Shadow Empire."

The barrier opened before Elena could respond. Not by force — by invitation. A section of the dark wall peeled back like a flower, revealing an interior so bright it hurt the eyes. Inside the barrier, countless ships floated — not weapons, not warships, but arks. City-sized vessels, each carrying thousands of Architects' descendants, preserved in stasis for two hundred million years, waiting for someone to wake them.

"The Architects didn't die," Elena whispered. "They slept."

The lead ark sent a transmission. It was a greeting — warm, curious, and carried in the Chorus frequency that the Architects and the Shadow Empire shared.

**"WELCOME, SINGERS. WE HAVE WAITED FOR THIS MOMENT. THE VOID WALKERS ARE CLOSE. PREPARE YOURSELVES."**

Elena felt something she had not felt since activating the first Gate on Mars: joy. Not the sharp joy of victory, but the deep, quiet joy of reunion.

The Architects were alive. And they were ready to fight.
