Chapter 7: The Alliance Council

The Free Star Alliance Council convened in the  Wanderlust'  s main cargo bay, converted into a temporary war room with holographic displays and borrowed comms equipment. Seventeen ships had responded to Jax Rainer's signal — a ragtag collection of cargo haulers, blockade runners, and converted mining vessels that looked more like scrapyard heaps than military assets.

They were the only defense humanity had.

"Status report," Jax said, standing at the center of the holo map.

Kira stepped forward. She was Alliance Navy, born on Ceres, and had defected after the Resource Wars when Earth had bombed a Belt wedding ceremony to make a point. She hated the Directorate with a precision that bordered on art.

"Mars is partially compromised." She tapped the display. The red planet glowed with blue nodes across its northern hemisphere. "The Bloom has spread to approximately forty percent of the surface. Nodes are activating at a rate of one hundred every hour. The population on Olympus Mons Station has been transformed — all two thousand of them. They're singing, Captain. That's the only word for it. They're all connected to the Chorus."

"How many are infected systemically?" Jax asked.

"About thirty percent show accelerated cognitive enhancement. The rest are… singing. They're not producing individual thoughts anymore. They're part of a distributed consciousness."

Jax felt a chill. Distributed consciousness meant hive mind. He had seen what that looked like during the Resource Wars, when the Directorate had experimented with neural linking for soldiers.

"And Earth?"

"Fleet Wing Seven is in orbit, immobilized. The Directorate has declared a state of emergency. They're calling the Bloom a biological weapon and threatening to deploy atmospheric suppression agents."

"Atmospheric suppression agents" was Directorate code for orbital nuclear strikes.

"Councilwoman Park is pushing back," Kira said. "She's arguing the Bloom is not a weapon but a transmission — that killing Mars will only guarantee humanity's extinction when the Void Walkers arrive."

Jax nodded at the display. "What about the Shadow Empire?"

"Nothing. They've gone dark. All their operatives have disappeared. The Belt intelligence networks are reporting complete silence."

Because they were already here, Jax thought. They were already inside the network.

"Captain." Dr. Elena Voss approached from the side of the cargo bay. She had changed since the excavation — her eyes held a new awareness, a depth that was both beautiful and terrifying. The Bloom had touched her but had not consumed her. She was the First Singer — the node that could control the network, that could speak for the Architects.

"We need to go to Mars," she said without preamble.

"Are you insane? Earth is about to glass the planet. Half the stations are already singing in some kind of hive mind. And you want to go back?"

"I need to reach the third Gate. The one at the south pole. It's the access point to Thalassa — the Architects' homeworld. If I can activate it properly, I can establish a permanent bridge. A highway. We can travel to their world, learn from them, prepare."

"Prepare for what?"

"For the Void Walkers." Elena tapped the display, bringing up the vision she had shared with the Architects' voice — the consuming darkness, the entropic wave that had already destroyed a civilization older than Earth. "They're coming. Six months, maybe less. And humanity is not ready."

Jax looked around the cargo bay. The Alliance captains — miners, smugglers, former naval officers — stared back at him. They were his people now. They had followed him because he promised them a future. Now he had to decide whether that future was worth dying for.

"Kira," he said. "Get the  Wanderlust  prepped for a Mars landing. We're going in."

"Captain —"

"I didn't ask for a debate. We're going in." He turned to Elena. "But we're not just going to sing for the Architects. We're going to make a deal."

"What kind of deal?"

"The kind where humanity gets the Bloom, but keeps its free will. The Architects gave us a gift. We're going to accept it — on our terms."

Elena smiled. It was the first genuine smile Jax had seen since the excavation, and it frightened him more than the alien ship.

"Good," she said. "Because the Architects are dead. But they left behind a choir. And you're about to become its conductor."
