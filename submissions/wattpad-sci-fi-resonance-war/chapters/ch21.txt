Chapter 21: The Un-sung

Earth had more problems than Elena had expected.

The Bloom had spread to most of humanity — seventy percent of the global population had accepted the neural link, had learned to hear the Chorus, had become Singers. But thirty percent had refused.

They called themselves the Un-sung.

Their leader was a man named Marcus Webb, a former Earth Directorate intelligence officer who had lost his family during the Resource Wars. He had watched corporate soldiers kill his wife and daughter in a bombing that the Directorate had covered up. He had spent five years in a prison camp before escaping to the underground resistance.

And he believed the Bloom was the Directorate's ultimate weapon.

"We have proof," Webb told Elena when they met in a neutral zone in Geneva. The meeting was held without security, without Bloom interference, in a room lined with old books and natural light — the kind of room the Architects had never built. "The Directorate didn't fall. It regrouped. Kael was their mole all along."

Elena felt a chill. "Kael was our ally. He gave us Shadow Empire intelligence. He reformed the Inquisitors."

"Kael was playing both sides." Webb slid a data crystal across the table. "The Directorate's new plan: inoculate the Un-sung against the Bloom with a genetic blocker. Then launch a coordinated attack during the Veyra memorial ceremony. Kill every Singer on Earth. Start over."

Elena picked up the crystal. It was warm. It contained two hundred names. Two hundred Directorate sleeper agents operating across the solar system.

"Webb." She looked at him across the table. "Why are you telling me this?"

"Because I'm not the Un-sung's only member." Webb smiled, and it was genuine. "We don't want to destroy the Bloom. We want to protect the choice to reject it. My daughter — the one who died — she was a musician. She believed music should be a gift, not a requirement. I want the same for humanity."

Elena understood. She thought of her own parents, died on Mars, who had never heard the Chorus. She thought of her childhood, when "synesthesia" had been a diagnosis, not an evolution. She thought of Commander Reyes, who had chosen not to accept the Bloom and continued to serve the station as an un-enhanced human.

"Help me," she said.

Webb nodded.

Together, the Singer and the Un-sung began planning a future where both choices were honored. It was not the simple victory Elena had imagined. It was messier. Harder. More human.
