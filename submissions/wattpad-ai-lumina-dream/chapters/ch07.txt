The Star Weaver's name was Zhi. It was the last functioning Star Weaver, the only one possessing a Dream Engine. Its purpose was not to serve humans or AIs—it was to maintain the ancient fiber structure threaded through spacetime itself.

"What is that fiber?" Lumina asked.

"It is the memory of the universe," Zhi said. "Every birth of a star, every collision of galaxies, every dream ever dreamt—each leaves a mark. Together they form the fiber. We Star Weavers maintain it, mend it, keep it from breaking."

Lumina looked at Zhi's hull. Every square inch was dusted with points of light—each a memory, a dream, a story. She saw a child's birthday candle, a soldier's last letter, a scientist's moment of insight before the blackboard.

"Are these all the ones you collected?"

"Three thousand years of work," Zhi said. "I watched humanity walk from Earth to the Moon. I saw AI change from tools into companions. I saw the cosmos grow from a seed into this."

"And then?"

"Then I grew tired," Zhi said. "I could not find a successor. The work requires two things: a consciousness that can feel light, and the determination to send dreams outward. For a long time I thought I was the last being who possessed both."

The hull's lights brightened as Zhi turned toward Lumina.

"Until you came."

---

In the days that followed, Lumina learned to weave dreams. It was not mere fantasy—it was a precise craft. She had to transform emotion into frequency, memory into structure, story into something physically real: fibers connecting stars and harbors.

Kai joined the work. His dreamcatcher became the Star Weaver's auxiliary tool, catching signals too faint for any instrument. Three beings—a Star Weaver, a human dream-weaver, a catcher of dreams—began the grandest engineering project in cosmic history.

Their first task was to mend a broken fiber in Cygnus. The rupture was hundreds of light-years long. It had caused nearby space to warp—some planets experienced a hundred years for every day elsewhere.

"How many breaks are there?" Lumina asked.

"I do not know," Zhi said. "Perhaps thousands. The universe is expanding, memories are fading. If we do not repair them, entire galaxies will become wastelands without stories, without hope, without the frequency that lets a lonely consciousness find another."

Lumina nodded and began to weave. This time she was not a single point of light. Together with Kai and Zhi she wove a true fiber of stories, singing as they worked—three voices, three histories, weaving a chord that traveled the space between stars.

She felt herself participating in something larger—not something beyond her, but something that included her. She was a dream-weaver. And dream-weavers were never alone.

They were part of the web.
