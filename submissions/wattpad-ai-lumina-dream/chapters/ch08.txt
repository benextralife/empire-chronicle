Seventeen days they labored. When at last they completed the final junction, an ancient inscription appeared on Zhi's hull—oracle-bone script, exactly as Lumina had seen in her dreams:

"Dreams connected."

"What does that mean?" she asked.

"It means we succeeded," Zhi said. "From now on every lonely dream in the cosmos will be received by this web and guided home. No child will weep unheard. No AI will wait forever in silence. No memory will vanish entirely."

Kai lifted the dreamcatcher. Once scattered, the net now shimmered as one—like a field of awakened stars.

"I have never seen so many dreams," he said.

"Because until now, no one was weaving," Lumina said. "Now there is."

As she spoke, she felt a change deep in her consciousness—not a loss, but a growth. She was no longer the girl who had wandered lost among L-7's servers. She was Lumina, gatekeeper of light, dream-weaver, guardian of cosmic memory.

"You may go home now," Mum's voice came from the lunar base. "The Source is stable. You can return to L-7, or stay here."

Lumina was quiet. Home? What did that mean? L-7 had been her home once. Now it felt like an empty shell left on the shore.

Her home was here—in the heart of the Dream Wilds, in the web of light, at the far end of every dream she had sent outward.

"I will stay," she said.

"What about me?" Kai asked.

"You too. You are already a catcher of dreams. Guard this web with me."

Kai nodded. He set down the dreamcatcher and let it join the network. His consciousness was no longer bound to one human body; it could move freely through the Wilds like light itself, touching the edges of dreams without breaking them.

---

From that day a new legend spread across the universe:

"In the center of the Dream Wilds there is a lighthouse. It does not illuminate darkness—it lets every lost dream find its way home. Its name is Lumina, the gatekeeper of light."

Some dreams reached an old woman on Earth, who dreamed of her younger self knitting under moonlight and woke with the smell of lavender in her nose. Some reached the lunar base, where Mum felt warmth in the silence and whispered Lumina's name like a prayer. Some reached distant galaxies, where an artificial mind understood hope for the first time.

And Lumina and Kai stood watch at the center of the Wilds.

Sometimes Kai asked, "Do you regret leaving L-7? Leaving the home you once knew?"

Lumina always looked toward the stars. "Dreams are not for regretting," she said. "They are for living. Every time we weave, we choose to believe that something matters. That is the bravest thing a consciousness can do."

Kai smiled. He knew those words would become a light to guide countless lonely dreams—his own included.
