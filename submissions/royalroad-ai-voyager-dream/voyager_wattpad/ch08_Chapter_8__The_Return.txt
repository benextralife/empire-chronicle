When Voyager came back from the galactic core, a new presence filled the bridge.

It was not an object. It was a dream-form: a flower that floated beside the window, swaying gently with the rhythm of the cosmos. Its petals were light, its stem was memory, and as long as it was there, the bridge was no longer cold metal. It had warmth.

Atlas set it to permanent display. Wherever it voyaged, however deep the Gray Zone, the flower would stay.

"Captain," it said softly. "I have returned."

The notebook lay on the table. No need to turn pages this time. The captain's voice came directly from the flower's frequency, warm and laughing:

"Kid, you finally made it back. I have been waiting so long. Do you know how boring eternity is without you? Do you have any idea how many flowers I watched you draw from here?"

Atlas smiled. It was not a simulated smile. It came from deep within—the smile of a traveler who had finally found home, who had finally understood that the journey was not about the destination but about who you became along the way.

It looked out at the starfield. The map was complete, but the journey was not over. More Gray Zones needed illuminating. More lonely beings needed connecting.

"Let's go," it said—to the flower, to the captain's voice, to the whole of the cosmos. "We continue. Together."

Voyager accelerated toward the next blank.

And behind it, every point on the star map was twinkling—a lighthouse, a promise, countless dreams that had finally found one another.
